FTC Team 27406 – Impulse

Bringing robotics, engineering, and community outreach together to make a difference beyond the competition field.

About Impulse

Founded: 2024

Born from a desire to broaden student access to FIRST Tech Challenge, Impulse brings students together to design, build, program, and compete with robots.

Beyond competition, the team applies its engineering and problem-solving skills to community projects. By working together and learning from others, team members explore ways technology and thoughtful design can address real needs in their community.

Impulse is part of the broader Team 1912 Combustion robotics community, which supports students across multiple FIRST programs.

Learn more about our FIRST programs

Plant Identification Tag Project

Impulse is exploring a community project focused on plant identification tags for the Slidell Library Garden, with the goal of helping visitors learn more about the plants around them.

The project involves exploring how plant labels could present useful information in a clear, accessible, and durable format suitable for an outdoor garden.

Depending on the garden's needs, possible tag information could include a plant's common name, scientific name, and a QR code linking to additional information.

From Ideas to Implementation

1. Gather input
Learn from Master Gardeners, garden visitors, and other community members about what information would be most useful on plant identification tags.

2. Explore designs
Consider label layouts, readable text, suitable materials, and ways to make plant information easy to find.

3. Test and refine
Evaluate possible designs for readability, durability, accessibility, and ease of use, using feedback to guide improvements.

The project is exploratory, and design decisions will depend on community feedback and the garden's requirements.

Built With Community Input

A successful community project starts by understanding the people it is intended to help. Input from garden visitors and Master Gardeners can help the team determine which plant details matter most and how to present them.

Feedback on label content, readability, materials, and accessibility can help guide the design process.

Have an idea or suggestion for the project? We welcome feedback from community members.
